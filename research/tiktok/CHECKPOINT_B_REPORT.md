## Findings (with confidence)

1. **The first seconds carry most of the impact.** TikTok's Creative Codes: 90% of ad recall and 80% of awareness
   impact within 6 s (A, verified in the PDF; TikTok gives no method). Product on a body in frame 1 is near-universal
   among better-ranked ads (36/38 vs 30/38, C).
2. **Talking beats silent montage; slower beats faster.** Better-ranked conversion ads more often have spoken
   narration (14/18 vs 8/18) and cut less (≈1.6 vs 2.6 cuts per 10 s) (C, directional). No credible evidence for
   "reset attention every N seconds"; fast cutting can lower engagement (B, partly a 2026 conference paper).
3. **Relevance, not entertainment, sells.** Fun or surprise unrelated to the product doesn't lift purchase intent
   (B). Likes don't track CTR rank in the sample (C).
4. **Callouts raise scrutiny, so proof must follow** (B: self-relevance increases elaboration). In the sample,
   callouts didn't change CTR rank on average, but broad situational callouts did fine and narrow problem
   callouts ranked low (C). Our callouts are broad situations.
5. **Our format is untested against the benchmark.** No AI-presenter ads in the sample, only 4 dresses, and only 6
   talking ads ≤ 20 s. Talking ads that ranked well ran ≈36 s (C, confounded).

Confidence: high on what TikTok documents; medium on peer-reviewed psychology applied to TikTok; low-to-medium on
the ad-sample patterns (curated sample, CTR rank only, confounded by objective).

## Options the evidence raises for the owner (not decisions)

1. **No on-screen text:** keep it; or test 3–5 key words in the first 3 s; or turn on TikTok's own auto-captions on
   the post.
2. **Callout as every first line:** keep it (≈8 words, situation visible in frame 1); or allow one demo-first test arm.
3. **15-second length:** keep it; or later test a 25–30 s version of a winner (~$8–10 per generation).
