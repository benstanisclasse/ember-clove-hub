## 3 places the evidence argues for changing a current rule (options for the owner, not decisions)

1. **No on-screen text.** TikTok recommends captions or overlays (A); same-language subtitles raised brand recall
   and intent even with sound on (B, Brasel & Gips 2014); muted viewers currently get no words at all. In the DB,
   text appears a little more often in better-ranked ads (23/38 vs 17/38, C), but full captions are rare (~10%) in
   both halves. *Options:* (a) keep the rule; (b) test 3–5 key words in the first 3 s only (PB H-AP-01);
   (c) turn on TikTok's native auto-captions on the organic post (default display and Spark behaviour still to be
   checked).
2. **Callout as the first line in every ad.** Callouts made no difference to CTR rank (9/38 vs 8/38, p = 1.0; within
   Conversions 7 vs 3, p ≈ 0.26, C). Broad situations ranked well and narrow body or health callouts ranked low, which
   matches the rule's "situation, never body" half. *Options:* (a) keep it, capped at ~8 words, with the situation
   visible in frame 1; (b) allow one demo-first or benefit-first opener (HK-04, HK-17) as a test arm against the
   callout control.
3. **15-second cap.** Better-ranked Conversions ads ran ~36 s vs ~17 s with slower cutting (1.6 vs 2.6 cuts per 10 s,
   C), but length is mixed up with talking-creator formats, and CTR rank is not cost per sale. *Options:* (a) keep
   15 s and never add cuts; (b) once a 15 s winner exists, test one 25–30 s version of it (~$8.10–9.72 per generation
   vs $4.86); (c) allow 3-shot or single-take cuts. Going past 30 s means joining clips, which conflicts with "used
   exactly as delivered".

**Also fix (no rule change needed):** drop the "skims instead of clinging" line for the fitted P01 (cling-averse
shoppers are a negative ICP for P01; "hangs, doesn't cling" is a P02-only idea). Each hook variant is a full
regeneration, so keep everything except the opening identical across test arms.
