## 1. TikTok testing campaign: settings (the $50 CBO test)

The written notes give no TikTok campaign settings at all; everything here comes from the videos.

- The default test, used by about 95% of students, is **one CBO campaign at $50/day (the CBO minimum), with one ad group holding 3 videos**. [TikTok Testing; ABO Testing]
- Before launch you need three things: a product that passes the criteria, a branded store, and 3 ads that fit the course's ad rules. Even the instructor says only about **1 in 5 products** he tests is a winner. [TikTok Testing]
- Objective: Website conversions. Campaign name: "[Product] CBO test". Ad group: "Ad group 1". [TikTok Testing]
- Pixel: **create a new pixel for every product** through the TikTok Shopify app. Disconnect the old pixel and create a new one before building the campaign. Then click around the store like a customer (add to cart, start checkout) to activate it. A "no activity" warning on a new pixel is normal. [TikTok Testing; TikTok Pixel]
- Optimisation event: **Complete Payment**. [TikTok Testing]
- Placement: manual, **TikTok only**. Untick Pangle, CapCut and the global app bundle. [TikTok Testing; ABO Testing]
- Advanced settings: **turn off comments, video downloads and sharing**. He says big brands do this too, so it does not look suspicious. [TikTok Testing]
- Location: **US alone** is the default. Adding Canada brings cheaper clicks that rarely buy. UK alone, Australia alone, or a US+UK+AU stack are the alternatives. [TikTok Testing]
- Gender: All, or **Female only for products that only women buy, such as women's clothing**. Never Male only. Age 18+. Language All. **No interests (broad)**. Ignore spending power and income. [TikTok Testing]
- Schedule: **start at midnight the next day**, so day one gets a full 24 hours. [TikTok Testing]
- Dayparting: with **one time zone, run 8am to midnight in that country's time**. With several time zones stacked, run all day. [TikTok Testing]
- **Smart creative ads: OFF for testing, ON for scaling.** [TikTok Testing; TikTok Scaling]
- Custom identity: store name plus logo on a white background, or the product photo as the avatar. [TikTok Testing] (He also suggests a made-up person's name with an influencer photo: see Do not use.)
- Ad text: **one line of text, the same line on all 3 ads** in the test. Always preview the URL. Ignore the new-pixel warning when submitting. [TikTok Testing]
- Account warm-up before the first real campaign: run a Traffic campaign at the $20 minimum budget and **switch it off after about $5 of spend**. Use automatic payments if offered. [TikTok Ads Walkthrough]

## 2. Alternate test: ABO

- Use this only after **2 to 4 products have failed in the CBO test**. [ABO Testing]
- Campaign "[Product] ABO test" with **CBO OFF** (leaving it on breaks the setup). Make **3 ad groups at $20/day each, with 1 video and 1 line of text per group**, so $60/day in total. Copy group 1 and swap the video for groups 2 and 3. [ABO Testing]
- Why use it: a CBO may spend $20 on one ad and a few cents on another. An ABO gives each ad the same spend, so the split test is clean. [ABO Testing]
- Countries for VIP students: **UK, Australia, New Zealand, Netherlands, Germany**, skipping the US (too competitive) and Canada (clicks and adds to cart but doesn't buy). Ads can stay in English. Later, change the store to suit whichever country brings the sales. Don't daypart a multi-country stack. [ABO Testing]
- The test's only job is to **find at least one winning ad**. One video is enough to move on to scaling. [ABO Testing]

## 3. Reading the test data: kill and keep checkpoints

- **Checkpoint 1, at $10 total campaign spend:** switch off any ad whose **CPC is above $1.50, unless it already has an add to cart (ATC)**. The target CPC is **under $1**. Between $1 and $1.50 can keep running. [Reading The Data] (The notes call under $1.50 "healthy" for the US. The video aims lower.)
- If every ad gets switched off for high CPC: **start a new test campaign tomorrow with 3 new videos**. [Reading The Data]
- **Checkpoint 2, at $20 total spend:** switch off any ad that has **spent $10 with no ATC**. An ad that has spent only $6 with no ATC keeps running. The max cost per ATC is **$10**. [Reading The Data]
- **Clicks under $1 but zero ATCs by $20** means an offer problem. **Relaunch the same ads tomorrow in a fresh campaign with a changed offer.** [Reading The Data]
- **Checkpoint 3, at $40 to $50 total spend:** judge profitability. **Any ad with a sale moves to the scale CBO.** [Reading The Data]
- An ad with no sale but **cost per ATC at 1/4 to 1/3 of the breakeven point** gets switched off in the test. **It still moves to the scale campaign**, because it showed buying intent. [Reading The Data; TikTok Scaling]
- No sales, ATCs too expensive, clicks still good: relaunch tomorrow with an offer change. CPC terrible: relaunch with new ads. [Reading The Data]
- Targets: cost per ATC between **1/4 and 1/2 of the breakeven point** (other videos say 1/4 to 1/3). Cost per purchase must be **below the breakeven point**. Expect **3 to 7 ATCs per purchase**. [Reading The Data; ABO Testing; Bid Caps]
- Fixes are always made by **relaunching a fresh campaign the next day**, not by editing the live one. [Reading The Data]
- Quote: "you only need one video to scale." [ABO Testing]

## 4. TikTok scaling: steady scaling with duplicate CBOs

- The day after the test: **if the test CBO is near breakeven or profitable, leave it running. If it lost $20 or more, switch it off.** Launch the scale campaign either way. [TikTok Scaling]
- Make a new campaign "[Product] CBO scale" with the **same settings as the test** and **Smart creative ON**. Use only the video(s) that got sales or many ATCs, with **5 different lines of text** (no duplicates). [TikTok Scaling]
- Launch **3 copies at $50 each rather than one at $150**. Not every copy performs. [TikTok Scaling]
- Read at **campaign level**. Near $10 spent, kill a copy whose CPC is way off from the others (e.g. $3 against under $1). By $20, kill any copy without ATCs in range. [TikTok Scaling]
- **Each day, duplicate every profitable campaign 2 to 3 times**: 1 becomes 3, then 9, then 20 or more. Keep the winners and trim the rest. [TikTok Scaling; Surf Scaling]
- ROAS bands: breakeven ROAS should be **1.75 or lower** (the notes say about 1.60). If it is above 2, rework the offer. A ROAS of 2 to 3 is enough to scale at about a 25 to 30% margin. **4 to 5 or more means duplicate aggressively.** [TikTok Scaling]

## 5. Surf scaling (a budget ladder within one day)

- **Double the CBO budget after every 3 profitable sales**: $50, then $100, $200, $400, $800, up to $2K or more. [Surf Scaling]
- You can start early **if the first 2 sales came in at about a $5 CPA**. [Surf Scaling]
- **After midnight, wait 1 minute, then reset every surfed CBO back to $50.** Start the ladder again each day, alongside the 2 to 3 new copies of yesterday's winners. [Surf Scaling]
- Before surfing, kill copies with a CPC way off from the others (e.g. $1.50 against under $1). Then kill any copy that reaches breakeven without a sale. [Surf Scaling]
- If doubling the budget kills performance (he says to try it on 2 campaigns, and stop if it fails on both), **keep the CBOs at $50**, try **+20% bumps** instead, or move to ABO scaling. He says surfing works for about half of accounts. [Surf Scaling]

## 6. ABO scale ("ABO spam")

- New campaign "[Product] ABO scale", CBO off, **ad groups at $20/day**, Smart creative ON, the winning videos, **3 to 5 text lines**. Start with **3 identical copies**. [ABO Scale]
- Read at **ad group level**. Kill the copy with the outlier CPC. Keep copies with ATCs in range and let them spend up to the breakeven point to get a sale. [ABO Scale]
- **When an ad group gets 2 to 3 profitable sales, duplicate it 2 to 3 times.** **Never raise an ABO's budget**; leave it at $20 and copy it instead (3, then 10, 30, 60). He says the small budget is what keeps ABOs consistent. [ABO Scale]
- Order of moves: duplicate CBOs and try surfing first. Use ABO spam if CBOs aren't working. Use bid caps last. [ABO Scale; Bid Caps]
- **Ads wear out after about 4 to 5 days (a week at most). Test new videos every 4 to 5 days** and build new scale campaigns from the winners. [ABO Scale; Bid Caps]

## 7. Bid caps (last resort)

- Use bid caps only when **CPC is good and cost per ATC is within 1/4 to 1/3 of breakeven, but you are only breaking even**. If CPC is above $1.50, test new ads first. If ATCs are weak, test a new offer first. [Bid Caps]
- Campaign "[Product] bids" with **CBO off** (bids can't run with CBO). **10 ad groups, each with a $200 daily budget, using Cost Cap bidding.** [Bid Caps]
- Bid levels: first bid = **breakeven point rounded up** (e.g. $20). Then **4 below ($19, $18, $17, $16) and 5 above ($21 to $25)**. Copies all show the first bid, so edit the bid column by hand after publishing. [Bid Caps]
- He says a bid above breakeven still tends to buy sales below the cap, and upsells add margin. [Bid Caps]
- Spend is slow: the budget looks like $2K/day, but **actual spend is about $100 to $200 on day 1**. [Bid Caps]
- Kill a bid with **no ATC at about $10 spent**. Let bids with ATCs spend up to the breakeven point, and switch off any with no sale by then. **Duplicate any bid with 2 to 3 or more profitable sales 3 times.** Don't surf bids. [Bid Caps]
- Our note: the $2K nominal daily budget exceeds anything approved. It needs the owner's sign-off even if real spend is lower.

## 8. Offers and pricing (Which Offer Is BEST, How To Create Each Offer)

- Weigh **most value** against **least resistance**. Example: BOGO at $40 gives more value, while a low price plus $9 shipping is easier to start. Expensive shipping can then cause friction at checkout. [Which Offer Is BEST]
- **Price at 3x COGS and make at least $20 profit per order.** On a $6 item, 3x gives only $12 profit, so go higher. [Which Offer Is BEST]
- Breakeven ROAS **about 1.6 is the most he will test at**. 1.2 to 1.4 is excellent. A $20-COGS item sold at $40 (breakeven ROAS about 2.0) is hard to make profitable. [Which Offer Is BEST]
- **Open every CJ supplier listing for the product in tabs** and compare cost, colours and sizes. Don't take the first one. [Which Offer Is BEST]
- **For the test, price at the low end of the pricing-table range** to prove demand first. [Which Offer Is BEST]
- Worked examples:
  - Sock clogs, COGS $18.29: sell one at $44.95 with no push to buy 2. Breakeven ROAS 1.68 is acceptable.
  - Silk bonnet, COGS $6.34 for 1 or $8.58 for 2: $19.95 plus $4.95 shipping with free shipping on 2+, or BOGO at $19.95 plus $9.95 shipping plus a free gift.
  - Shaver, COGS $11.21: $29.95 plus $4.95 shipping. Breakeven ROAS 1.59. [Which Offer Is BEST]
- Summary: COGS $6 to $13 suits **BOGO or free shipping on 2+**. COGS $15 to $18 suits **free shipping on everything plus a free gift**. Above $20, skip the product. [Which Offer Is BEST]
- Shipping example: **free above a threshold (e.g. $35), $4 to $4.95 below it, plus an express upgrade** ($4.95 to $7.95). [How To Create Each Offer; Shipping & Delivery]
- Use the bulk editor to enter price, compare-at price and **cost per item (CJ cost) for every variant**. [How To Create Each Offer]
- Our caveat: the course's breakeven formula covers product cost only. It leaves out payment fees, returns and app costs.

## 9. Advanced offers, bundles, AOV

- Add a **free complementary gift costing about $1 to $2.50** that CJ ships **in the same package**. It adds only cents to shipping. Price from the combined COGS using the pricing table. The Kaching Bundles free-gift option handles it. [Advanced Offers]
- He sometimes **makes the first tier deliberately weak** so buyers choose a higher tier (a decoy). [Advanced Offers]
- CJ is preferred over AutoDS because CJ combines shipping when items share a box. [Advanced Offers]
- Kaching bundles: label tiers **Buy 1 / Buy 2 / Buy 3** (never Single/Duo/Trio). Discounts of **10% and 20%**, free shipping from 2, a "Most popular" badge on Buy 2, and **Buy 1 preselected** so upgrading feels like the buyer's choice. Let customers pick variants inside the bundle and hide the separate variant picker. Send bundle clicks straight to checkout, unless a cart-upsell app is installed. [Bundles]
- Cart add-on: **priority processing at $2.95** and a free-shipping progress bar. Install only once there is sales volume. [Upcart]
- Post-purchase one-click offer: the **same product at 10% off, then 35% off** as the downsell. He says **10 to 20% of buyers accept**. [Aftersell]
- Order tracking and chargeback apps only after **$1K+ days**. He says a dropshipper from China loses about 50% of disputes, plus a fee of about $40 each. [Tracktor; Disputifier]

## 10. Winning product selection (Winning Product Mastery)

- **"Winning audience" idea:** buyers want the outcome, not the item. Products with the same outcome share an audience. For women's fashion, "more confident in my clothes" links shapewear bodysuits, BBL-illusion jackets, butt-lifting leggings and flare jeans, shapewear-lined sundresses, and dresses that create an hourglass shape. If one of these went viral, a new item with the same outcome is a strong candidate. [Winning Product Mastery]
- Fashion examples that sold: fleece-lined tights and jeans (warm but still stylish), teddy hoodies, hoodies with message designs on the back, floral hourglass dresses, satin night-out dresses. [Winning Product Mastery]
- **Seasonality:** dresses sell in spring and summer. Fleece and teddy items sell in winter. Gifts (jewellery with a note, promise necklaces) only around the holidays. [Winning Product Mastery]
- **"Saturation is a myth"** as long as a competitor is running ads now and there is recent content. **If there have been no new videos or sellers for about a year, skip it.** [Winning Product Mastery]
- Quick checks: search TikTok and look for **videos with 20K+ views or likes**. You need **5 to 10+ usable videos**. The competitor store must look polished and be active. Revenue spy tools are a rough guide only. [Winning Product Mastery]
- Avoid: replicas or dupes (illegal), skin-toning devices, adult products, alcohol or tobacco (**even a tiny bottle in the background can get an ad flagged**), weighted hula hoops and belly-toning creams (ban risk), phone accessories, drones and projectors (nobody is scaling them), products with copyright issues. [Winning Product Mastery]
- From the research-tool videos [PipiAds; WinningHunter]:
  - PipiAds filters: TikTok, Shopify, last 30 days, **10+ ads**, **50+ likes**, countries US/UK/CA/AU.
  - Signs of a spammy store (don't follow it): emojis or hearts in product titles, very long titles, "last day" in the name, many countries targeted.
  - WinningHunter: **an ad running 7+ days** is a good sign. Ads only hours old prove nothing yet, so wait or find another competitor. Sort by ad spend. Shoes are risky because of sizing.
  - Skip Valentine's products: Chinese New Year closes suppliers and gifts arrive late.

## 11. Kalodata (TikTok Shop spy tool)

- Products tab, sorted by revenue as a starting point. **Don't choose by revenue.** A product on a dip is still fine if it is evergreen, in a proven niche, has margin and has lots of content. Check the 30-day revenue chart. [Kalodata]
- Use **Categories** to keep beauty, health and fashion, and exclude men's subcategories when targeting women. [Kalodata]
- Then search the product on PipiAds (**10+ ads**) to study competitors' offers, stores and ads. Then use **CJ image search** to get COGS. [Kalodata]
- **You don't have to match TikTok Shop prices.** A $17 Shop item can sell for $40 to $50 with a high-AOV offer. [Kalodata]
- His three main criteria are **proven niche, margins and plenty of content**. Active competitors are a plus. He says TikTok is where trends start and Meta amplifies them. [Kalodata]
- (Kalodata lets you download creators' videos; whether those can legally be used as ads is covered in Do not use.)

## 12. Ad creatives: basics, do's and don'ts

- Ad structure: **the problem in the first 1 to 2 seconds**, then the product as the solution, then **3 to 4 benefits** (how, where, what, who), then a **CTA**. The ad's only job is the click; the store does the selling. [Ad Creatives Basics]
- Formats: **UGC-style** (one person, natural) or **compilation** (several clips, more polished). Neither wins by default, so **test both**. Use whatever the product has the most good footage for. [Ad Creatives Basics]
- When researching other ads for reference, prefer **videos posted in the last 1 to 3 months** with strong engagement (about **20K likes** is "a lot"). **Likes aren't cash**: skip foreign-language, low-quality supplier, branded or spammy videos even if they have views. [Ad Creatives Basics]
- A competitor running **the same video in many ad copies** is a sign they are scaling it. [Ad Creatives Basics]
- Vary the 3 test ads: mix sources, one high-view and one low-view style, one compilation. Each ad is a chance to reach a different buyer. If all three look alike, they fail together. [Ad Creatives Basics]
- **On-screen text must look native to TikTok.** No neon or hazard-style text boxes. The ad must make sense **on mute**: caption every spoken line. [Ad Creatives Basics]
- Captions [CapCut]:
  - **2 to 4 words per line**, always one line.
  - **No gaps in the caption track**: a caption is always on screen.
  - Bold sans font (Rubik Bold), uppercase, about 120% scale, stroke and shadow.
  - Cut clips on voiceover lines, with a new clip at least every 1 to 2 lines.
  - Background music low, at about 30 to 40% volume or less.
  - The end-card CTA can use a black box on the lower third. [Copy_Paste]
- Image ads (Meta): only when scaling or out of video. Use real product photos with the logo added, not Canva designs. [Ad Creatives Basics]
- Start with simple ads. **Make custom content once a product is proven and profitable.** [Ad Creatives Basics]

## 13. Store quality (Good vs Bad Stores, Store Design)

- Bad stores:
  - no effort on the description, or a text-only one;
  - default theme with **white buttons and a white announcement bar**;
  - **blurry photos**;
  - every element in one colour so nothing stands out;
  - **unrelated upsells**;
  - a logo that doesn't fit the product, or a spammy name;
  - **several announcement bars**. [Good vs Bad Stores]
- Good stores: sharp photos, a friendly font, emoji benefit bullets, a **GIF at the top showing the product in use**, benefits, before/after, FAQ, reviews, "what's included". It reads like a real brand. [Good vs Bad Stores]
- **The first image should show a person (face or body) using the product, not the bare item.** People buy the result. [Good vs Bad Stores]
- **One announcement bar, stating the main offer** (e.g. free shipping on orders over $X). [Good vs Bad Stores]
- If you use his pre-built theme, use it **as supplied**; don't bolt on extras. Three tabs: How it works / Guarantee / Shipping. Check the page on mobile, since most TikTok traffic is on phones. **Update guarantee and shipping text for each product.** [Good vs Bad Stores; Store Design]

## Applies to our next test ($50/day, 3 ads, one product)

1. **Build it as the course's CBO test:**
   - Website conversions, CBO $50, 1 ad group, 3 videos.
   - Complete Payment on a **new pixel made for this product** (activated by test clicks before launch).
   - TikTok-only placement, US only, Female (it's women's fashion), 18+, broad.
   - Start at midnight Eastern the next day, daypart 8am to midnight ET.
   - Smart creative off. Comments, downloads and sharing off.
2. **Before launch, write down the breakeven point, breakeven ROAS, max cost per ATC (breakeven/4 to breakeven/3) and max CPA (= breakeven).** Don't launch if breakeven ROAS is above about 1.75, or if profit per order is under $20.
3. **Kill and keep checkpoints on campaign spend:**
   - **$10**: switch off any ad with CPC above $1.50 that has no ATC.
   - **$20**: switch off any ad that has spent $10 with no ATC.
   - **$40 to $50**: any ad with a sale, or with cheap ATCs, goes to scaling.
   - Log each checkpoint in the daily check.
4. **Diagnose, then relaunch fresh the next day, never mid-flight:**
   - Expensive clicks: 3 new ads.
   - Cheap clicks but no ATCs: same ads, new offer.
   - Good clicks and ATCs but no sales: offer or checkout problem.
5. **Make the 3 ads genuinely different in their first 1 to 2 seconds and in format** (e.g. a talking presenter, a try-on montage, a detail/fit close-up). Each follows problem, then solution, then 3 to 4 benefits, then CTA. Native TikTok text, captions of 2 to 4 words per line with no gaps, readable on mute. All are our own AI-presenter footage, labelled as AI.
6. **Ad text: one benefit-led line, the same on all 3 ads** (e.g. outcome plus "Shop now"). Unlike the course, **no prices, "today only" or "few left" in the ad line or captions**. The price lives only on the product page.
7. **Offer:** price near 3x COGS with at least $20 profit per order. For a dress at COGS under $13, free shipping on 2+ (Buy 1/2/3 with 10%/20% off, Buy 1 preselected) fits the course and our plan. A cheap same-box gift is an option. Price at the low end of the range for the test.
8. **Have a scale plan ready, but it needs owner approval before any spend above $50/day:**
   - Test losing $20 or more: switch it off.
   - Winners go into 3 copies of a $50 CBO with 5 distinct benefit lines and Smart creative on.
   - Copy whatever is profitable 2 to 3 times each day.
   - Optional surfing (double the budget per 3 profitable sales, reset to $50 at midnight). Fall back to $20 ABOs if surfing hurts results.
9. **Plan the next batch of 3 ads now:** the course says winning ads wear out in 4 to 5 days.
10. Custom identity: **Ember & Clove name and logo** (or a product photo). Not a person's name or face.

## Do not use

Each item below breaks platform rules, misleads buyers, risks copyright claims, or breaks an Ember & Clove owner rule.

- **"Copy-paste" ad strategy: DO NOT USE.** Downloading other people's viral TikToks, then trimming a frame off each end, shifting the colour, and layering a copy of the audio at -59 dB so duplicate-content checks can't match it. [Copy_Paste] This evades platform detection and re-uploads other creators' work without permission. Also covers "ripping" competitor ads [TikTok Testing], downloading creators' videos from Kalodata, PipiAds or WinningHunter to run as ads [Kalodata; Ad Creatives Basics; Ad Checklist in the notes], and the claim that reusing unbranded TikTok videos counts as fair use [Ad Creatives Basics]. That claim is wrong: unlicensed reuse in paid ads is infringement and breaks TikTok's ad policy.
- **Fake reviews: DO NOT USE.** Generating 24 reviews with ChatGPT, inventing reviewer names, mixing in a few bad ones to look real, and taking AliExpress buyers' photos as "customer" photos. [ChatGPT Reviews] He says "you can't just make up reviews" and then shows how anyway. Also on-page testimonials with invented names and cities, and **AI images of "someone holding the product" presented as customer photos**. [Store Design] This breaks FTC rules and our no-testimonials rule.
- **Testimonial-style voiceovers: DO NOT USE.** The CapCut example script is a first-person "since I got it… I've even lost weight" testimonial. [CapCut] This breaks our no-testimonials rule, and for weight claims, TikTok policy too.
- **Fake scarcity and urgency: DO NOT USE.**
  - Ad lines like "few units remain" and "ends at midnight" for a sale that never ends. [TikTok Scaling]
  - A permanent "50% off today only" announcement bar. [Store Design; Page Templates] The course's own Meta notes say to avoid this phrase.
  - Copied offers of the "next 30 minutes" / "next 100 orders" type. [Advanced Offers]
  - A 10-minute cart countdown timer that resets. [Upcart]
  - "Only X left" stock counters (in the notes).
  - Fake price anchors: a made-up compare-at price set at double the real price to show "50% off". [How To Create Each Offer]
  - Also, prices and "today only" in ad text break our no-prices-in-captions rule.
- **Bait-and-switch shipping: DO NOT USE.** A low headline price ("$15 today only") with a $14 shipping charge revealed only at checkout. [TikTok Testing] This is a misleading pricing pattern. Shipping must be clear before checkout.
- **Posing as a person: DO NOT USE.** Setting the TikTok identity to a made-up person's name with an influencer's photo so the ad looks like a creator posted it. [TikTok Testing] It misleads viewers and conflicts with our labelled-AI-presenter rule.
- **Celebrity likeness: DO NOT USE.** He notes a competitor's hit ads used Joe Rogan footage, which he himself calls illegal. [Winning Product Mastery] Never use a celebrity's face or clips.
- **Getting around bans: DO NOT USE.** Buying ad accounts on Fiverr, buying "reinstated profile" setups, proxies or anti-detect browsers to hide from a past ban, a new domain after a ban, using a family member's Meta profile. [Profiles/Agency Accounts/Bans in the notes; the TikTok Ads Walkthrough also mentions banned accounts] This breaks TikTok and Meta terms and can get the business banned for good.
- **Using an agency account to dodge VAT: DO NOT USE** without an accountant. The course pitches agency accounts as a way to avoid the 20% UK/EU VAT. [Notes; ABO Testing] VAT is owed on sales whatever ad account is used.
- **Shady product claims: DO NOT USE.** Health products pitched on unproven claims ("hydrogen water", "grounding" sheets, "healing" copper bottles). [Winning Product Mastery] These invite ad rejections and FTC risk. Not relevant to us, but do not copy the angle.
- Judgement call: **hiding comments** [TikTok Testing] is allowed by TikTok, but it removes buyer feedback. The owner should decide; we should still read the comments.
