## A. Executive summary

**What the product is.** A loose, chunky rib-knit pullover with a folded turtleneck and dropped shoulders, cut long enough to wear as a very short dress over tights (about 26.8–27.6 in from shoulder to hem). Fibers are acrylic and polyester with an angora wool blend; percentages are not stated. Seven colors, burgundy featured. $44.99 plus $8 shipping (free over $70), delivered in about 2–3 weeks from overseas warehouses, 7-day returns at the buyer's cost.

**Three findings that change the brief.**

1. **The "S–3XL" size range is probably not a plus-size differentiator. [V] for the math, [I] for the conclusion.** The supplier's garment chart lists bust 112–122 cm (44–48 in) and length 68–70 cm across all six sizes, so each size step adds only about 2 cm (0.8 in) of bust. A US 2X *body* bust is 49–53 in and a 3X is 53–57 in on Nike's chart [O], with Kohl's listing 3X at 51.5–55.5 in [S]. The "3XL" garment (48 in) is therefore smaller than the body of a typical US 2X or 3X shopper. It would sit close or tight on them, not slouchy. In practice this is one oversized silhouette in six labels: very roomy on US S–M bodies, relaxed on L–XL, and doubtful beyond about US 1X. The approved ad's line "sizes small to 3X" invites the shoppers most likely to be disappointed, under a buyer-pays-return-shipping-to-overseas policy. **Recommendation: drop "to 3X" from ads until a 3XL sample is measured, and do not run a plus-size angle yet.**
2. **Length is the biggest product risk. [R]** At about 27–28 in, this is shorter than every comparable product measured: Amazon/Nordstrom/Madewell/Quince/Eloquii comparables run 31–39.5 in (competitor table, section C). Reviewers already call 32 in "short" at 5'9" [O]. The product wears as a tunic or micro-mini. It works only over opaque tights or leggings, and is a poor fit for women about 5'7" and taller. Ads should show it with tights and boots every time, and never imply it is a stand-alone dress.
3. **The style is commoditized and price-anchored. [V]** Near-identical oversized turtleneck sweater dresses sell on AliExpress for about $5–20 [O], on SHEIN for about $11–20 [S], and on Amazon (Pink Queen, S–3XL, 31–36.6 in long, Prime shipping) for around $46 [O]. Ember & Clove's delivered price is $52.99 with 2–3 week shipping and no reviews. The ads cannot win on price, speed or social proof, so they have to win on **making the outfit feel decided** and on **honest specificity**.

**The most compelling customer needs found (by evidence strength).**
- An outfit formula for cold weather: sweater dress + tights + boots is the dominant search pattern. "sweater dress tights" autocompletes first to "and boots" (Google, fetched today) [V][R].
- Comfort that still reads as "dressed": "as close as you can humanly come to wearing pajamas to work" (Cap Hill Style) [R].
- Fear of cling: clingy, bodycon sweater dresses are a top complaint, especially from curvy women [R]. Note the counter-fear: chunky, boxy knits can look like a "house" [R].
- Holiday-eating comfort ("cookie season", "clothes to eat turkey in") [R, moderate].
- Distrust of social-ad stores: shipping from China, "keep it for 15%" refund offers, items that don't match the ad [R].

**Most promising segments (detail in E/F/K).**
1. **Outfit-formula seekers:** want a no-thinking cold-weather outfit (tights + boots).
2. **Comfort-first "pajamas that pass" dressers:** casual days, errands, WFH, weekend plans.
3. **Holiday-season eaters:** want nothing tight at the waist for Thanksgiving through December dinners. Time-limited by shipping.

**Strongest initial angle opportunities.**
- **First test: "The whole outfit is three pieces: tights, boots, this."** (outfit formula, A2).
- **Backup: "Pajama-comfortable, still looks dressed"** (A1).
- **Time-boxed opportunity: "Holiday dinner dress, nothing tight at the waist"** (A4, only while delivery can still land before the occasions).
- **Trust test for round 2: "Three honest things before you order"** (A8). It addresses the length, fiber and shipping objections head-on, which no competitor does.

### C2. Substitutes and alternatives
- **Cheaper substitutes:** SHEIN, Temu, AliExpress, Walmart marketplace ($5–42). YouTube "HIT or MISS" Temu/SHEIN sweater-dress hauls draw 33k–66k views [O, titles/view counts].
- **Same price, faster, with proof:** Amazon brands with thousands of ratings and Prime shipping (Pink Queen, ANRABESS, ZESICA).
- **Premium:** Madewell, Quince, Aritzia, Free People, which offer natural fibers and longer lengths.
- **DIY / do nothing:** an oversized sweater the shopper already owns, worn with leggings. "Long sweater + leggings" is the default habit. A 27-in "dress" is functionally close to it [I]. This is the biggest inertia force.
- **Bought alongside:** opaque or fleece-lined tights, knee-high boots, ankle boots, slips, belts (styling articles and forums [O]/[S]).

### C3. What competitors emphasize, and what customers say
- **Overused claims [V]:** "oversized/relaxed/flattering for all body types"; "chunky knit keeps you warm"; "hides arms and belly"; "pair with leggings and knee-high boots"; long occasion lists; "anti-pilling"; huge color counts (13–31 on Amazon vs 7 here); free shipping and long return windows.
- **Repeated complaints [R]:** too short or "a sweater, not a dress"; runs big ("frequently returned due to loose fit"); itchy, especially at the turtleneck; neck too tight or "choking"; pilling and shedding; static cling; thin or see-through in light colors.
- **Repeated praise [R]:** not itchy; thick and cozy; perfect with boots and tights or leggings; roomy and forgiving.

### C5. Switching and objections
- **What would make a shopper choose this over Amazon:** seeing it styled exactly the way she wants to wear it (outfit decided), a color she wants, honest sizing info, crossing the $70 free-shipping line with two colors [I].
- **What would make her choose a cheaper alternative:** recognizing the style from SHEIN/Temu/Amazon; the 2–3 week wait; $8 shipping on one item; no reviews [I], supported by delivery-expectation and dropshipping-complaint evidence (D7).
- **Objections even when it looks right:** "too short for me", "is it itchy", "will it be see-through or thin", "which size", "returns are a hassle" [R].

#### A8: Three honest things before you order (transparency)
1. **Angle:** pre-empt the top objections and use honesty as differentiation.
2. **Segment:** S1/S2 shoppers who are wary of ad stores and AI ads.
3. **Insight:** shoppers report deceptive social-ad stores ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker/lookupscam/1099702), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.cayred.com?page=2) [O]). 54% trust brands less when ads are visibly AI [O]. 4. **Situation:** sees a polished AI ad and suspects a catch.
5. **Motivation:** avoiding a regretted purchase.
6. **Jobs:** emotional (trust, control).
7. **Why this product:** the store's own facts (length, fiber, shipping) are specific and can be stated.
8. **Feature:** exact length; fiber list; shipping window; tights styling.
9. **Promise:** "You'll know exactly what you're getting."
10. **Evidence:** pain [R]; effect on conversion [H].
11. **Objection:** honesty may reduce click-through or conversion.

#### A3: Hangs, doesn't cling
- **Insight:** cling is the most repeated fit frustration (D1). **Caveat:** curvy women with a body bust above ~44 in will get little ease from a 48 in garment, and some fear "boxy". The angle must not say "flattering on every body" or "hides your tummy". Demonstrate the drape instead.
- **Hook:** "If sweater dresses always cling on you, watch how this one hangs." **Creative:** side-profile turn and walk; the presenter pinches the fabric away from her waist; tights and boots. **Hypothesis:** body-relief framing outperforms outfit framing among women who stop on it. Watch return requests for "too small/too big".
- **Why rank 2–3:** strong pain, but the product proof depends on unmeasured real-world fit, and an AI presenter of one body type cannot honestly show range [I].

## K. Ranked priorities

### K1. Scoring method
Each angle or segment is scored 1–5 on the ten criteria from the master prompt: **Ev** strength of evidence; **Sev** severity/importance of the problem; **Out** strength of the desired outcome; **Urg** urgency of the trigger; **Rel** relevance of *this* product to the need, including fit and length limits; **WTP** plausible willingness to pay; **Reach** ease of reaching the audience on TikTok with spoken 15-s AI-presenter ads; **Diff** differentiation from competing offers; **Demo** ability to demonstrate credibly under the ad rules; **Comm** commercial attractiveness given the $31.13 break-even and $21.79 target CPA (including return risk and AOV).

**Notes:** WTP has **no product-specific data**. It is scored 2 across the board on the evidence that the style sells at $5–58 (C1); "U" marks are used where nothing at all is known. Scores rank experiments; they are not predictions.

## L. Testing roadmap

### L1. Ground rules for all tests
- **Message tests** (round 1): same audience (broad US women 18+, no interest stacking, so the algorithm finds buyers [I]), same placement, same budget per ad, same landing page, same price and offer, same presenter, set and burgundy colorway where possible. **Only the angle (hook + middle line) changes.**
- **Audience tests** (later): hold the winning message constant and vary audience (e.g., interest groups or lookalikes once there are purchasers). Keep these separate from message tests.
- **Primary metric:** cost per purchase vs **target CPA $21.79** and **break-even $31.13**, then contribution per order (including the $8 shipping revenue and any free-shipping two-unit orders). Clicks alone don't decide.
- **Secondary diagnostics:** 2-s view rate and 6-s view rate (hook strength), average watch time, CTR, cost per landing-page view, add-to-cart rate, checkout-initiated rate, CVR, AOV (share of two-unit orders), refund/return requests and the reasons given, size distribution of orders (watch 2XL/3XL share).
- **Stop-loss (economics, not statistics):** pause an ad that spends about 1–1.5× break-even CPA (~$31–47) with no add-to-cart, or about 2× break-even with no purchase. Judge only after each ad has had comparable spend. No sample sizes, confidence levels or expected results are assumed, because no baseline exists.
- **Before any spend (owner, outside this report's scope):** add inch measurements and fiber info to the page; remove "to 3X" from the approved ad's spoken line, or re-record; confirm the TikTok AIGC disclosure is on (TikTok Ads Help lists it as mandatory for AI-generated media [O]).

### L2. Round 1: message test, same audience (3 ads)

| | Test 1 | Test 2 | Test 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Outfit-formula framing yields the lowest cost per purchase | Comfort framing beats outfit framing because comfort is the deeper motive | Occasion framing lifts conversion during the holiday window |
| **Segment** | S1 | S2 | S3 |

| **Creative** | Pull-on over tights → boots → fold neck → mirror, side profile | Couch → stand → slouch demo → coat → out the door | Set table → stand → drape → burgundy + tights + boots |

**Control:** keep the approved blended ad (with "to 3X" removed or re-recorded) as a fourth ad if the budget allows. It shows whether single-message ads beat the blended one.

### L3. Round 2 (after a Round 1 winner exists)
1. **Trust layer test (A8):** the winning angle's ad vs an A8 "three honest things" version, same audience. Primary: CPA; secondary: return and refund requests per order, and "too short" mentions. Supports if CPA is within about 10–15% of the winner while complaints and returns are lower. The tolerance is a judgment call for the owner, not a statistical threshold.
2. **Body-relief test (A3):** A3 vs the winner. Watch the size mix and fit-related returns closely.
3. **Retargeting offer (A9):** site visitors and add-to-carts get "Burgundy or sky blue? Get both and shipping's free." Primary: AOV and two-unit order share; secondary: CPA.
4. **Color test (A5):** burgundy vs black vs sky blue on the winning script. Note: gray, khaki and sky blue lack search demand [V], but icy blue has 2026 trend coverage [O].

### L4. Research and optimization sequence
1. **Now:** order a size S and a 3XL sample. Measure bust, length, neck opening and opacity (white, sky blue); check itch, shedding and pilling after a wash; get fiber percentages from the supplier. This decides the fate of A7/S6, A3 and several page claims.
2. **Now:** update the product page with inch measurements, the "made for tights and leggings" note, fiber disclosure and the plain shipping and return terms. This is the cheapest conversion and return-rate lever.
3. **Weeks 1–2:** Round 1 message test. Read TikTok ad comments daily as a substitute for the Reddit/TikTok VOC this research could not access, and log objections verbatim in the Hub.
4. **Weeks 2–4:** Round 2. Begin collecting **real** post-purchase feedback (never fabricate). Ask buyers for height and size to build honest fit guidance.
5. **After the season peak (Jan):** reassess. Sweater-dress search interest falls to near zero by May–Aug per a third-party Google Trends summary [O, Accio], so do not plan spend beyond about February.

## M. Research limitations and open questions

**What remains unknown**
- **All Ember & Clove customer data:** no sales, reviews, returns or ad results exist. Every segment and angle is a hypothesis about *this* store's buyers.
- **Real garment fit:** the supplier size chart is the only source. Real bust, length on a body, neck opening, opacity, itch, pilling and shedding are unverified. The 3XL conclusion depends on the chart being accurate.
- **Fiber percentages:** the angora share is unknown. This affects itch and shedding risk and what can be said.
- **Willingness to pay:** no product-specific data; scores use competitor price bands as a proxy.
- **Platform VOC:** Reddit (all subreddits named in the brief), TikTok comments, YouTube comments, Quora, BabyCenter and What to Expect could not be accessed. Lulus, Free People, Aritzia, Zara, H&M, Walmart and SHEIN review text was blocked. VOC therefore leans on Rent the Runway, YouLookFab, Nordstrom, J.Crew, Corporette, Cap Hill Style, Mumsnet, Trustpilot and BBB. These skew older and more style-forum than TikTok's audience [I].
- **Time-sensitive trends:** burgundy trend evidence is mostly 2023–2025 coverage seen in search; 2026 color coverage is mixed (Apparel Resources burgundy [O]; Refinery29 icy blue and chocolate [O]).
- **Seasonality data** is from a third-party summary of Google Trends (Accio), not Google Trends directly.
- **AI-ad trust surveys** are vendor or agency surveys (Net Conversion; Clutch [S]); treat them as directional.

**Conclusions that are hypotheses**
- That outfit framing (A2) converts better than comfort (A1) or occasion (A4) framing.
- That transparency (A8) reduces returns without hurting CPA.
- That petites are a good-fit niche (width may still swamp them).
- That the product does not cling on S–XL bodies (inferred from garment bust vs typical body bust).

**Data that would most improve confidence (in order)**
1. Measured S and 3XL samples, plus fiber percentages.
2. Round-1 TikTok results (hook rate → CVR → CPA) and verbatim ad comments.
3. The first real buyers' height, size and satisfaction (collected honestly).
4. Accessible Reddit/TikTok threads on oversized sweater dresses (manual browsing by the owner).
5. Google Trends direct comparisons (sweater dress vs turtleneck sweater dress vs oversized sweater dress).

**Note on the master prompt:** the final quality-standard checklist ended mid-sentence ("The highest-priority angles have…"). The available checklist items were applied. The missing remainder could not be.

**Quality check against the available standard:** product-specific ✔; segments have distinct motivations ✔ (E, last paragraph); ICPs cover who and why ✔; triggers and alternatives covered ✔; customer language sourced ✔ (with access limits stated); angles are motive-distinct ✔; features linked to outcomes ✔ (B2); recommendations weighted by evidence and economics ✔ (K); claims within page support ✔ (H rules).
