# Pickleball Accessories as a Category Brand with Recurring Revenue (US, as of Oct 19, 2026)

Scope note: Builds on Checkpoint 1 (SFIA 24.3M players in 2025, +22.8%; 7.48M core; USAP spin test from Oct 1, 2026; paddle delistings; no dedicated ball/overgrip subscription brand found). About 27 search/fetch calls were made. Many accessory price and durability figures come from retailer pages, affiliate blogs and user reviews. Their reliability is flagged throughout. Anything marked **[EST]** is the researcher's own estimate, with inputs shown. These are not sourced facts.

## 1. Product ladder: hero, adjacent, consumable, premium (retail and sourcing prices)

### Takeaway
A workable ladder exists: bag or backpack as the hero ($40–$150), then edge guard/lead tape and accessories, then overgrips and outdoor balls as consumables, with premium bags or apparel at the top. But every rung is already sold at low prices by Franklin, JOOLA, Selkirk and Amazon. The consumables are cheap, commoditized, multi-pack items, so the brand would have to earn its margin on the bag/hero tier, not on consumables. Paddles add certification cost and delisting risk, and they should stay out of the entry ladder.

**Paddles: certification cost and process (why not to lead with paddles)**

- UPA-A (pro play, PPA/MLP): the 2026 annual brand fee was cut from $20,000 to $10,000. Certification is $3,000 per paddle, valid 24 months, with an 8-week standard turnaround. - USAP has de-certified and delisted paddles (JOOLA; "quietly delists several paddle models") and runs a new spin-rate test (2,100 RPM threshold discussed) — [USA Pickleball on JOOLA](https://usapickleball.org/news/usa-pickleball-statement-on-decertified-joola-paddles-and-equipment-testing-standards); [The Dink delistings](https://www.thedinkpickleball.com/usa-pickleball-quietly-delists-several-paddle-models/); [Pickleball Effect spin test](https://pickleballeffect.com/resources/usaps-new-spin-rate-test/); [The Dink on spin test certification problem](https://thedinkpickleball.com/why-usaps-new-spin-test-presents-a-bigger-certification-problem/)

### Inferences
- **[EST] Suggested ladder.** Hero: a style-forward or "organized" backpack or sling at $59–$99, which demos well on video (paddle sleeve, fence hook, ball pocket, cooler or shoe compartment). Adjacent: edge guard plus lead tape kit ($12–$20), towel, fence hook, ball holder clip ($10–$25). Consumable: overgrips (3–6 pack, $8–$15) and outdoor balls (6–12 pack, $12–$25). Premium: a leather/canvas "lifestyle" tote ($120–$200) or apparel. Paddles come later, if ever. Their inputs are a $1,500+ USAP fee per model, about 6 sample paddles, 4–9 weeks of testing, and ongoing delisting/spin-test risk. On top of that, buyers are trading up to Selkirk/JOOLA (Checkpoint 1).
- **[EST] Margin math on the hero bag.** Inputs: a China custom bag FOB of perhaps $8–$18 (assumption, since no quote was found), about 24% effective tariff, and $2–$4/unit freight. Landed cost would be about $12–$26 against a $69–$99 retail, which leaves room for Meta CAC. This is the only rung with obvious gross-margin headroom.
- Consumables are a weak margin engine. Franklin X-40 regularly sells at about $1–$2.50 per ball, and overgrips at about $2.30–$2.70 each. A DTC brand cannot charge much above those anchors without a functional edge.

### Gaps
- No verified AliExpress/AutoDS prices were found for bags, nets, ball retrievers or knee supports, and no confirmed FOB quotes for anything. Overgrip prices are Alibaba listing prices only.
- Portable nets, ball pickup tubes, paddle erasers, sunglasses, court shoes and knee/elbow supports were not price-checked in this pass.
- USAP's 2026 fee schedule was not confirmed on usapickleball.org, and the $1,500 figure may be out of date.
- No wholesale (distributor) cost for Franklin X-40 or Dura Fast 40 was found.

## 2. Category ownership: who owns the lifestyle, pricing, subscriptions, whitespace

### Takeaway
No one owns the "accessories lifestyle." Performance brands (Selkirk, JOOLA, Franklin) own credibility and sell bags at $40–$222. Franklin dominates outdoor balls through the X-40, which is sold everywhere and heavily discounted. Small lifestyle bag brands (Ame & Lulu, Georgie & Lou, Think Royln, GearPickle) target women on style. The one consumables-subscription-like offer found is The Dink's quarterly mixed box ($99, 2023 data). No active pickleball ball or grip auto-ship brand was confirmed. Retailers (PickleballCentral) use rewards and credit, not autoship.

### Inferences
- **Whitespace (Plausible-untested).** (a) Style-forward, organized bags and accessories for women 45–70, especially Sun Belt retirees. They are the most frequent players (core skews older) and the most active on Facebook. (b) "Outdoor-only player kit" bundles (balls, grips, towel, cooling, sun protection). (c) Auto-replenishment for consumables. That last slot is empty, but it may be empty because the economics are poor (see Section 4), not because nobody thought of it.
- **Moat risk.** Franklin can undercut on balls at any time, and JOOLA/Selkirk can launch bags and colorways at scale. A new brand's defensible asset would be design, community and content around a demographic, not product IP.
- The Dink's box suggests media/community brands, not gear brands, are the natural owners of a "membership" offer. A new DTC brand would compete with them for attention.

- It is unconfirmed whether Amazon Subscribe & Save is offered on Franklin X-40 or overgrip listings.

## 3. Ball and grip consumption and spend per player

### Takeaway
An avid outdoor player plausibly goes through one ball every 15–50 sessions, depending on surface and temperature (cold accelerates cracking 2–3x). They replace an overgrip every 2–4 weeks at 3x/week. Core-player survey data puts ball spend at about $110/yr. **[EST]** The total consumables "wallet" (balls plus grips) for an avid core player is about $140–$190/yr, or roughly $12–$16/month, which caps any consumables subscription.

### Cited Findings
**Ball lifespan (mostly anecdotal and retailer-blog sourced)**
- Franklin X-40 at 3x/week on rough outdoor concrete: expect a crack "every 15–25 sessions", and 35–50 sessions on smoother sealed surfaces. Below 50°F the plastic gets brittle, and northern players report cracking "2–3x faster in October through March" (the FAQ on the same page says about 2x, so the page contradicts itself). Selkirk Pro S1 is claimed to last 35–50 sessions. Note that FORWRD is a gear retailer/blog — [FORWRD X-40 review 2026](https://forwrd.co/blogs/gear-guides/franklin-x-40-pickleball-review-2026-the-official-tournament-ball-honestly-rated)
- Anecdotes vary widely. One player says 3.5+ players "crack them in a few games" and a ball lasts "a few weeks". A Slickdeals commenter guesses about 50 games. A casual buyer reports 4–5 sessions of 7–10 games each — [search summary of Slickdeals/retailer reviews](https://slickdeals.net/f/17904213-12-count-franklin-sports-x-40-outdoor-pickleballs-ember-11-99-free-shipping-w-prime-or-on-35)
- Dura Fast 40 reviews: one group with five dozen balls playing 6 days/week had 6 cracked balls in 6 weeks. A Fort Myers player buys a dozen 2–3 times a year. Several reviews say the ball "break[s] too easy in cool weather", and some report going out of round within one game — [PickleballCentral reviews](https://pickleballcentral.com/Dura_Fast_40_Outdoor_Pickleball_p/pb111.htm)
- X-40 is reported as more cold-sensitive (cracking below about 50°F). Dura is harder and holds up better in heat but is brittle in cold. Mayfair Farms reports "fewer cracks than any other ball" for X-40 over about 60 hours (affiliate source) — [Pickleball Effect ball test](https://pickleballeffect.com/equipment-reviews/best-pickleball-balls-tested-ranked/); [Mayfair Farms](https://www.mayfairfarms.com/best-pickleball-balls/)
- No controlled or lab crack test from Franklin, Dura or USAP was found. A claimed "10% stiffness loss per hour" figure has no cited study and should be disregarded — [search results summary; see Pickleball Gang](https://thepickleballgang.com/best-outdoor-pickleball-balls/)

**Overgrip replacement at 3–4x/week**
- Every 3–4 sessions, so about every 1–1.5 weeks at 3x/week — [FORWRD grip replacement guide](https://forwrd.co/blogs/gear-guides/pickleball-grip-replacement-guide)
- Every 8–15 hours of play for sweaty players — [MyPickleballConnect](https://mypickleballconnect.com/guides/pickleball-overgrip-and-grip-sizing/)
- Every 3–4 weeks for 3–5x/week players — [The Dink](https://www.thedinkpickleball.com/how-often-should-you-change-your-pickleball-paddle-overgrip/)
- At 1–4 hours/week, every 2–3 weeks; at 5–10 hours/week, weekly — [Pickleball Union](https://pickleballunion.com/how-often-to-re-grip-your-pickleball-paddle/)
- An outlier guide says every 3–4 months — [FORWRD](https://forwrd.co/blogs/gear-guides/how-to-replace-pickleball-paddle-grip)

**Spend per player (survey data)**
- The SFIA x DUPR 2025 Pickleball Market Playbook surveyed n=1,102 DUPR-database players aged 13+, fielded Apr 22–Jun 3, 2025, all core players. Over 90% play at least twice a week. Average annual spend: paddles $318, court shoes $240, balls $110, with equipment overall above $600. Facility fees are $913, lessons $688 and tournament fees $517. Over 90% plan to keep or increase equipment spend — [The Kitchen Pickle](https://thekitchenpickle.com/blogs/news/pickleball-player-spending-habits-equipment-membership-dues/); [DUPR playbook](https://www.dupr.com/pickleball-state-of-the-market-consumer-habits-report); [SFIA release](https://sfia.org/resources/sfia-and-dupr-team-up-to-provide-industry-first-pickleball-market-playbook-with-unmatched-insights-on-player-spending-and-playing-habits/)
- Accessories and apparel spending were not reported in the summaries.

### Inferences
- **[EST] Annual consumables wallet for an avid (3x/week) outdoor player:**
  - Balls: DUPR survey $110/yr. At about $1.80–$2.50/ball (100-pack retail), that implies about 45–60 balls/yr, or about 4–5 per month. The DUPR sample skews competitive/tournament, so the casual core figure is likely lower.
  - Overgrips: inputs are 3 sessions × 50 weeks = 150 sessions/yr, with a replacement every 6–12 sessions (2–4 weeks). That gives 13–25 grips/yr at about $2.30–$2.70 each (3-pack retail), or about $30–$68/yr.
  - Total is about $140–$180/yr, or about $12–$15/month at retail. This is the ceiling a consumables subscription competes for, and much of it is already captured cheaply on Amazon and Walmart.
- Balls are often a shared/group purchase (one ball in play among four players; clubs supply balls). Individual-level demand for frequent ball shipments may be lower than crack rates suggest. This pushes ball subscriptions toward the B2B/club side.
- Seasonality cuts both ways. Cold raises crack rates (more consumption per session), but northern players play outdoors far less in winter. Sun Belt heat reportedly softens balls ("mushiness"), so replacement drivers exist year-round there.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer (Franklin, Dura/Escalade) or USAP lab data on lifespan in games or hours was found.
- No Pickleheads spend survey was found. No casual-player (1–7x/yr) spend data was found.
- No data was found on how many balls/grips an average player actually buys per year (units), only dollars from the DUPR sample.

## 4. Subscription deep dive (Phase C1)

### Takeaway
Of the four offers, **club/league bulk supply (B2B)** has the clearest functional need and the best shipping economics, but it is price-competitive against Franklin and PickleballCentral and is exposed to club financial stress (Picklr Ch. 11). **Ball and grip auto-ship (D2C)** is Plausible-untested. It has real recurring consumption, but small baskets ($12–$25), heavy shipping and Amazon price anchors make contribution margin thin unless shipments are quarterly. **Membership (content plus discounts)** and **seasonal gear refresh** are Speculative, and media brands (The Dink, DUPR+, Pickleball360) already occupy that space. **[EST]** Use subscriptions as an LTV add-on attached to a hero bag purchase, not as the business model.

### Cited Findings (inputs used in estimates)
- Retail anchors: X-40 at about $0.80–$2.50/ball depending on deal or pack size; overgrip 3-packs $6.95–$7.99 — sources in Section 1.
- Alibaba overgrip listings $0.12–$0.55/pc (not FOB-verified) — [Accio/Alibaba](https://www.accio.com/business/best-pickleball-overgrips)
- Consumption: X-40 cracks every 15–50 sessions; overgrips last 2–4 weeks at 3x/week; core ball spend is $110/yr — sources in Section 3.
- Precedents: The Dink quarterly box $99 (2023); Qranc monthly tennis-ball subscription at a 5% discount; Tennis Trunk monthly balls-only (2016) — sources in Section 2.
- Club stress: Picklr filed Chapter 11 on Oct 9, 2026 (N.D. Texas). It has 70+ US locations, about $19M liabilities, and a 2025 net loss of $4.4M on $10.3M revenue. The cause was lease guarantees on about 40 franchise locations. A $4.5M financing commitment was made — [The Kitchen Pickle, Oct 16, 2026](https://thekitchenpickle.com/blogs/news/picklr-bankruptcy-filing-indoor-pickleball-facilities/); [Racket Business, Oct 12, 2026](https://racketbusiness.com/p/pickleball-s-players-keep-coming-why-are-some-clubs-going-broke)
- 26.9% of sport/fitness participants say out-of-pocket costs affect their participation (SFIA mid-year research) — [Racket Business](https://racketbusiness.com/p/pickleball-s-players-keep-coming-why-are-some-clubs-going-broke)

### Inferences (all unit economics are [EST]; assumptions shown)
Common cost assumptions (none verified with quotes): private-label outdoor ball landed cost $0.70–$1.20 (China FOB plus 24% tariff plus freight); overgrip landed $0.35–$0.80; 3PL pick/pack $3.00–$4.00 per order; packaging $0.50–$1.00; USPS Ground Advantage for a 1–2 lb parcel $6–$9 (2–4 lb $9–$12); payment fees about 3%. De minimis is suspended, so all units must clear as formal/informal entries with duty paid. That favors bulk import to US stock, not AutoDS dropship, for consumables.

**A. Ball + grip auto-ship (D2C) — Plausible-untested**
- **What they get:** 6 outdoor balls plus 3 overgrips (monthly), or 18 balls plus 9 grips (quarterly). Choose ball type (fast/hard for heat, softer for cold), grip color and tack.
- **Why keep paying:** never caught without a ball or fresh grip; climate-tuned ball selection; a consistent "brand ball" for their group.
- **Cadence and price:** monthly at $24.99, or quarterly at $59.
- **Variable cost, monthly at $24.99:** COGS 6×$0.95 + 3×$0.55 = $7.35; pick/pack plus packaging $4.25; shipping about $7.50; fees $0.75. Total about $19.85, so contribution margin about $5.14 (about 21%).
- **Variable cost, quarterly at $59:** COGS 18×$0.95 + 9×$0.55 = $22.05; pick/pack plus packaging $4.75; shipping about $10.50; fees $1.77. Total about $39.07, so contribution margin about $19.93 (about 34%), or about $6.64/month. Quarterly is clearly better.
- **Retention drivers:** frequency of play, sweat level, outdoor-only play, group "ball bringer" role.
- **Cancellation reasons:** oversupply (balls pile up because groups share), cheaper Amazon/Walmart multipacks ($1/ball deals), winter layoff, injury, quitting or slowing play, ball preference (many groups standardize on X-40 or tournament-required balls).
- **Inventory/supplier needs:** US-stocked private-label balls (USAP-approved ball listing needed for credibility in sanctioned play — not verified), consistent quality (out-of-round complaints are common), and an overgrip OEM.
- **Automation:** Shopify Subscriptions/Recharge/Skio, skip/pause and "swap to winter ball" flows, SMS reminders.
- **LTV levers:** cross-sell the hero bag, edge tape and paddles from partner brands; add-on carts.
- **Fatigue risk:** high. Commodity product, low novelty.
- **Seasonality:** northern players pause November–March. Sun Belt is steadier but heat softens balls.

**B. Seasonal gear refresh (quarterly box, about $59–$79) — Speculative**
- **What they get:** a curated seasonal kit (spring: grips, balls, sunscreen sleeve; summer: cooling towel, visor, hard balls; fall/winter: indoor balls, grips, warm layers).
- **Variable cost at $69:** COGS about $18–$25; pick/pack plus packaging about $5; shipping about $10; fees about $2. Total about $35–$42, so contribution margin about $27–$34 (about 39–49%).
- **Retention drivers:** novelty and perceived value ("$X of gear for $69").
- **Cancellation reasons:** clutter ("I don't need another towel"), poor fit or style, value skepticism. Generic subscription-box fatigue is a known pattern, though no pickleball-specific churn data was found.
- **Supplier needs:** many SKUs and MOQs, so inventory risk is high.
- **Precedent:** The Dink's $99 quarterly box (2023), current status unknown.
- **Fatigue risk:** very high.

**C. Membership: clinic/drill content + member discounts ($8–$15/month or $79–$129/year) — Speculative for a gear brand**
- **What they get:** drill library, beginner-to-3.5 progression plans, monthly live Q&A with a coach, 15–20% off gear, free shipping, early access to drops.
- **Variable cost:** near zero per member besides content production (fixed: coach fees, filming) and discount margin give-up.
- **Retention drivers:** skill progression (DUPR rating gains), community and local meetups.
- **Cancellation reasons:** free YouTube content, competing paid platforms (Pickleball360, DUPR+), plateau or quitting.
- **Note:** this is closest to "proven" in adjacent digital form (DUPR+, Pickleball360 exist). Proof as a gear-brand bolt-on is unverified.

**D. Club/league bulk supply (B2B) — Plausible-untested (functional need is real, economics price-competitive)**
- **What they get:** 100-ball boxes plus grips/towels on a monthly or quarterly schedule, branded balls with the club logo, net/court accessories, net-30 invoicing.
- **Price anchor:** X-40 100-pack at about $180–$250 retail; a B2B offer might land at about $120–$160 per 100 for private label.
- **Variable cost per 100-ball box:** COGS about $70–$120; freight about $15–$25.
- **Contribution margin:** about $10–$60 per box. Thin, but low CAC once a club is signed.
- **Retention drivers:** convenience, logo branding, reliable supply.
- **Churn and risk:** club failure or receivables risk (Picklr Ch. 11 shows the stress); franchise systems often lock in official ball sponsors; price shopping.
- **Seasonality:** indoor clubs peak in winter, which offsets D2C winter weakness.

**Overall labels**
- No pickleball consumables subscription was found to be operating at scale, so none can be called **Proven-in-category**.
- The closest proven analogs are adjacent: media boxes (The Dink), tennis ball subscriptions (Qranc, Tennis Trunk) and digital memberships (DUPR+).

### Gaps
- No actual pickleball subscription churn/retention data, no 3PL quotes and no USPS rate verification were gathered. All contribution-margin figures are illustrative.
- It is unverified whether private-label balls need USAP approval to sell credibly, and the cost of ball approval was not researched.
- B2B club purchasing behavior (who buys, at what price, under which sponsorship contracts) was not researched.

## 5. Participation durability: slowing or declining in 2026?

### Takeaway
Pickleball participation reached a high base (24.3M in 2025). But growth decelerated sharply to **+1.3% in H1 2026** (SFIA mid-year, as reported secondhand). Attention metrics are negative, and leveraged facility operators are failing (Picklr Ch. 11). Meanwhile, pro-tour owners raised $225M in May 2026 at a $750M valuation. The picture is a durable sport that is plateauing, not a collapsing fad. For an accessories brand, that means a large installed base with replacement demand, but no tailwind of new players to mask weak CAC.

### Cited Findings
- 2025: 24.3M players (+22.8%); casual players 16.8M (+23.9%); core players 7.48M (+20.4%); about 4.5M new players in 2025; up from 4.2M in 2020; 5th straight year as fastest-growing sport. The SFIA 2026 Topline was released Mar 12, 2026 — [SFIA single sport report](https://sfia.org/resources/sfia-releases-2026-pickleball-single-sport-report-team-sports-reports-to-follow/); [Pickleball.com](https://pickleball.com/news/sfia-report-confirms-over-24-million-americans-playing-pickleball); [The Dink](https://www.thedinkpickleball.com/report-24-million-americans-are-now-playing-pickleball-up-171-in-just-3-years/)
- SFIA's mid-year release (August 2026) reports pickleball growth "moderated to 1.3%" in H1 2026. Padel grew 14.5% — [Racket Business, Oct 12, 2026](https://racketbusiness.com/p/pickleball-s-players-keep-coming-why-are-some-clubs-going-broke). Caveat: this is secondary. The SFIA First Half Game Plan report is members-only, available per the [SFIA Sept 2, 2026 newsletter](https://sfia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/9-2-nl.pdf), and the primary figure was not seen directly.

### Inferences
- **Durable, not a fad.** Evidence for durability: 7.48M core players, an older core that plays multiple times a week, 70k+ courts installed (sunk infrastructure), institutional capital into the pro tour (Apollo), and the DUPR finding that 90%+ of core players plan to keep or raise equipment spend. Evidence of saturation: +1.3% H1 2026 growth, negative attention index, facility bankruptcies, and an older pattern of casual players churning (the casual tier is 69% of players).
- **Implication for this brand.** A replacement-cycle and accessories business benefits from a large existing base even if growth is flat. But the "new player starter kit" angle loses tailwind. The paddle trade-up dynamic and USAP spin-test/delistings may create a moment of paddle churn (Checkpoint 1) that accessories can ride (new grip, new edge tape, new bag).

- No 2026 court-construction pace (new courts per year) was found.

## 6. Creative angles and audience targeting (older demographics, Meta CPM)

### Takeaway
The highest-frequency buyers skew older (about half of core players were 55+ in 2021 SFIA data; 27.8% of all 2025 players are 55+) and increasingly female (42.9%). That favors Facebook and Instagram over TikTok for the core consumables buyer. TikTok may suit the younger, casual, style-driven hero-bag buyer. No reliable 2025–2026 age-specific Meta CPM data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Age and gender figures (27.8% aged 55+ in 2025; 52% of core aged 55+ in 2021; women 42.9%) — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/family-relationships/pickleball/); [Florida DEP fact sheet](https://softlive.floridadep.gov/sites/default/files/2022-Pickleball-Fact-Sheet-updated-5.5.22.pdf); [Pickleball.com](https://pickleball.com/news/sfia-report-confirms-over-24-million-americans-playing-pickleball)
- Teens and young adults (13–24) have the highest participation rates of any age group in 2025. Average player age is about 35, per an APP report cited by AARP — [Pickleball.com](https://pickleball.com/news/sfia-report-confirms-over-24-million-americans-playing-pickleball); [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/family-relationships/pickleball/)
- US Facebook audience share: 55–64 is 15.1% and 65+ is 8.1%, and the 45+ share has grown 12% since 2023. Global average CPM is about $10–$15, with no age split (secondary stats aggregators) — [Searchlab Facebook ads statistics 2026](https://searchlab.nl/en/statistics/facebook-ads-statistics-2026); [AffNinja](https://affninja.com/facebook-ads-statistics/)
- Older (early-2010s) Nanigans data showed the 45–54 and 55–64 groups monetized best, with higher CPCs for older groups. This is outdated — [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/online/facebook-ads-women-older-age-groups-monetized-best-in-q1-42097/)
- 26.9% of participants say out-of-pocket costs affect participation, which makes value messaging relevant — [Racket Business](https://racketbusiness.com/p/pickleball-s-players-keep-coming-why-are-some-clubs-going-broke)

### Inferences
- **[EST] Creative angles:**
  1. "Everything in one bag" organization demo (paddle sleeve, ball tube, fence hook, cold-drink pocket), aimed at women 45–70 on Facebook/IG Reels.
  2. "Your ball cracked again?" problem/solution for outdoor players, showing cold-weather cracking and a "climate-matched ball" auto-ship.
  3. "Regrip in 60 seconds" tutorial (satisfying ASMR-style video for TikTok/Reels), with an overgrip pack as the entry SKU or bundle add-on.
  4. Spin-test or delisting news hook: "Is your paddle still legal? Protect your new one" (edge guard, cover), riding the October 2026 USAP spin test.
  5. Sun Belt community and social proof (retirement community courts, couples' matching gear).
- **[EST] CPM implication (assumption, not data).** Older audiences on Facebook are commonly assumed to have higher conversion intent and competitive CPMs. Medicare/insurance/finance advertisers bid heavily for 55+ in Q4 (Oct–Dec, Medicare open enrollment). That could inflate CPMs for 55+ targeting during the first test window. This should be validated with test campaigns. It is not sourced.
- TikTok reaches the younger, casual segment, which has lower purchase frequency. Use it for hero-bag or style creative, not consumables auto-ship.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 Meta or TikTok CPM/CPA by age data was found. Agency benchmarks or Meta's own reach planner would be needed.
- No data was found on pickleball ad performance or ROAS benchmarks for accessory brands.
