# DIY Car Detailing as a Category Brand with Recurring Revenue (US, as of Oct 2026)

Research notes for Checkpoint 2. Researched 2026-10-19. Builds on Checkpoint 1: Chemical Guys' 20% monthly subscriber conversion (2022, company figure), Adam's reliance on Amazon Subscribe & Save (gallons ~$26.99), and SEMA's $52.65B in 2024 accessories/mods spend.

Labels used:
- **[SOURCED]**: from a cited page.
- **[ESTIMATE]**: my arithmetic on sourced or stated inputs, with the inputs shown.
- **[ASSUMPTION]**: unsourced, to be verified.

Several sources are deal aggregators (Slickdeals, Dealnews) or third-party estimators (Grips, GummySearch), so their price and traffic figures should be treated as indicative only.

## Q1. Product ladder: hero, adjacent, consumable, premium (retail and sourcing prices)

### Takeaway
The demo-able heroes are the foam cannon and, more strongly, the cordless pressure washer with a foam pot. Both are already commoditized on TikTok Shop and Amazon at $17–$90, and the market leaders sell foam cannons at about $100. The recurring revenue is in the liquids (shampoo, wheel cleaner, quick detailer, ceramic spray at $10–$35 per bottle), but Amazon Subscribe & Save and Walmart already discount these heavily. A new DTC brand's margin must come from bundling and brand, not from scarcity.

### Inferences
- **Proposed ladder [ESTIMATE / design]:**
  - **Hero ($49–$129):** cordless washer plus foam cannon starter kit, or a "no-hose" rinseless kit (bucket, grit guard, mitt, towels, rinseless concentrate).
  - **Adjacent ($20–$60):** towel packs, wheel brushes, drying towel, interior kit.
  - **Consumables ($10–$35):** shampoo or rinseless, wheel cleaner, quick detailer or ceramic spray, interior cleaner, tire dressing, glass.
  - **Premium ($120–$250):** DA polisher plus pads, compound and polish.
- **Hero price gap:** A $99 branded cannon competes with $12–$18 generics on Amazon, so price-sensitive TikTok buyers can easily trade down. The hero therefore needs a visible point of difference: battery-powered, no pressure washer needed, or bundled with consumables that make the second order natural.
- **The cordless washer is the stronger demo product** (the TikTok sold counts are an order of magnitude above standalone foam cannons). But it is a China-sourced electronic item: battery/UN3480 shipping, warranty returns, and the roughly 24% tariff all apply. It also does not create a consumable lock-in on its own.
- **Microfiber is a high-margin adjacent item** (about $0.41–$0.58 landed for wholesale 16x16 versus about $6/towel retail for The Rag Company). It also has a natural replacement cycle.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable AliExpress/1688 unit prices for foam cannons, cordless washers, wash mitts, buckets or grit guards. Supplier quotes are needed.
- I found no current 2026 official price lists for Adam's, Griot's or Chemical Guys gallons. Direct fetches of brand product pages returned 404s.
- I found no data on attach rates (what share of hero buyers buy consumables).

## Q2. Category ownership: who owns the enthusiast identity, and where is the whitespace

### Takeaway
Chemical Guys is still the best-known "lifestyle" brand. It is privately owned by AEA Investors since July 2021, with Golub Capital mezzanine lenders. I found **no evidence it was acquired by Monomoy**, and no confirmed sale through Oct 2026. Its DTC site looks weak in 2025–2026 (third-party estimates show declining online GMV, and core soaps were sold out on its own site), and some Detail Garage franchise stores have closed. The enthusiast tier is fragmented across Adam's, Griot's, The Rag Company/Obsessed Garage, CarPro and others. The clearest whitespace is the "no hose / apartment" rinseless-washing customer, but Optimum No Rinse already owns that product among enthusiasts.

## Q3. Subscription deep dive (Phase C1)

### Takeaway
The only proven recurring model in the category is chemical replenishment via Amazon Subscribe & Save (5–10% discounts, used by Adam's, Chemical Guys, Obsessed Garage and ONR). Chemical Guys' own DTC subscription (Ordergroove, launched 2022, ">20% monthly subscriber conversion", a vendor-published company claim) appears not to be prominently offered on its site in Oct 2026, which is a caution signal. Usage math works against frequent auto-ship: a gallon of concentrate lasts a home washer many months. Realistic cadences are 60–120 days, with strong winter seasonality.

### Cited Findings

**Wash frequency and where people wash**
- **AmericanMuscle survey, June 2026** (1,005 US car owners):
  - Americans wash "about every 2 weeks" on average. 1 in 10 never clean their car.
  - 61% of Dodge/BMW owners and 65% of coupe/sports-car owners wash at least every 2 weeks, versus 36% of sedan owners.
  - Only 27% of Tesla owners clean every couple of weeks.
  - 52% have been embarrassed by a dirty car.
  - — [AmericanMuscle](https://www.americanmuscle.com/how-often-americans-wash-their-cars.html)
- **Most US washing happens at professional washes, not at home:**
  - ICA's 2019 US study (1,800 respondents): a record 83% washed at a professional car wash in 2019, and DIY washing declined. — [ICA](https://www.carwash.org/car-wash-news/ica-releases-u-s-car-wash-consumer-study)
  - ICA 2018: 30% of US washers wash most often at home. — [via AmericanMuscle search snippet / ICA](https://www.americanmuscle.com/how-often-americans-wash-their-cars.html)
  - 2023 trade data: 89% used a professional wash. — [autoserviceworld](https://www.autoserviceworld.com/?p=218076)
  - These are older data (2018–2023), flagged.

### Offer-by-offer analysis
All economics below are **[ESTIMATE]** with inputs shown. Shipping and fulfillment inputs are **[ASSUMPTION]**: I did not retrieve current USPS/UPS rate cards.

**A. Chemical replenishment auto-ship. Label: Proven-in-category** (Amazon S&S at Adam's/CG/ONR; CG DTC 2022)
- **Customer receives:** shampoo or rinseless concentrate, plus one or two rotating items (quick detailer or ceramic spray, wheel cleaner, tire dressing, glass), in 16 oz bottles or a gallon refill.
- **Cadence math:**
  - Home washer, bucket wash, every 2 weeks = 26 washes/year. Shampoo use is [ASSUMPTION] about 1–2 oz per wash (bucket), more for a foam cannon at [ASSUMPTION] about 2–4 oz per fill. That is about 26–100 oz/year, so one 16 oz bottle lasts about 2–8 months and a gallon about 1.3–5 years.
  - ONR at 1 oz per wash [SOURCED dilution]: a gallon is about 128 washes, or about 5 years at 26 washes/year.
  - **So the natural cadence is quarterly or slower for casual users, and roughly 60 days for enthusiasts using foam cannons.** A monthly cadence would oversupply.
- **Plausible price [ESTIMATE]:** a $35–$45 quarterly box (shampoo 16 oz plus two 16 oz detail sprays), priced against retail of about $10–$17 per 16 oz bottle (Adam's, CG above) less 10–15%.
- **Variable cost [ESTIMATE], about $20.20 per box:**
  - Product COGS: 3 × 16 oz private-label bottles at about $5–$10 each (Renegade: $299–$600 for 60 filled, labeled 16 oz bottles, so about $5–$10/bottle at minimum volume; falls at scale), about $15–$30. Midpoint about $20.
  - Box and dunnage [ASSUMPTION] about $1.50.
  - Pick/pack [ASSUMPTION] about $3.
  - Ground shipping for about 4 lb [ASSUMPTION] about $9–$12. Non-flammable water-based liquids are not hazmat. Flammable or aerosol items require Limited Quantity ground.
  - Payment fees about 3% (about $1.20).
  - **Total about $34–$36 midpoint on a $40 box. Contribution margin is about $4–$6 (10–15%).** This only works at scale with lower COGS (about $3/bottle would give about $12 CM, roughly 30%).
- **Retention drivers:** convenience; "matching system" lock-in (the brand's soap fits the brand's foam cannon); free gift with box 3.
- **Cancellation reasons:** overstock from over-frequent cadence (the most likely cause, given the usage math); winter; switching to cheaper Amazon gallons; washing at a car wash instead.
- **Inventory/supplier:** a US blender (see Q4) at 60-bottle MOQ per SKU (Renegade) or 50-unit MOQ (Ethos); 3PL with liquids capability.
- **Automation:** Shopify subscription app (Recharge, Ordergroove, Skio); skip/delay emails tied to season.
- **LTV levers:** gallon refills (lower shipping per oz); add-on carts; cadence nudges from weather or zip code.
- **Fatigue risk:** high, because consumption is slow.
- **Seasonality:** winter in snow states likely cuts DIY washing [ASSUMPTION; no seasonal data found].

**B. Seasonal maintenance kit (quarterly). Label: Plausible-untested** (no player found offering it)
- **Customer receives:**
  - Spring decon kit: iron remover and clay.
  - Summer kit: ceramic spray and UV protectant.
  - Fall kit: sealant and glass.
  - Winter kit: rinseless wash, salt/undercarriage cleaner, interior mats cleaner.
- **Why keep paying:** novelty plus season-appropriate needs, which addresses the winter churn of Offer A.
- **Price [ESTIMATE]:** $49–$69 per quarter.
- **Costs [ESTIMATE]:** similar to Offer A plus one more SKU, about $25–$35 COGS plus about $14 ship/pack. CM is about $10–$20 on $59. SKU sprawl raises MOQ cash needs (each new SKU means another 60-bottle minimum at Renegade).
- **Risk:** the customer may not use the previous kit before the next arrives (backlog churn).

**C. Microfiber replacement. Label: Plausible-untested** (The Rag Company sells bulk packs, but I found no subscription)
- **Customer receives:** a 6–12 towel refresh every 6 months.
- **Rationale:** towels degrade with washing [ASSUMPTION; no usage-life data found].
- **Price [ESTIMATE]:** $25–$35 semiannually.
- **Costs [ESTIMATE]:** COGS about $0.50–$1.50/towel China-sourced (US wholesale is $0.41–$0.58 for basic 16x16 [SOURCED]; premium edgeless 500gsm costs more [ASSUMPTION]). The 24% tariff applies.
- **Margin:** shipping is light and non-hazmat (about $5–$7 [ASSUMPTION]), so CM is high (about 50–65%).
- **Risk:** low perceived need. Better as an add-on to Offer A than as a standalone.

**D. Membership (discount + education + early access). Label: Speculative** (no detailing brand found running a paid club)
- **Customer receives:** about 15–20% off, free shipping, how-to video library, community, members-only drops.
- **Price [ESTIMATE]:** $49–$79/year.
- **Variable cost:** low (content plus the discount cost).
- **Risks:** Chemical Guys' engagement model has been a giveaway ("$1 = 1 entry"), not a paid club; free YouTube/TikTok tutorials undercut paid education; heavy discounting erodes already thin chemical margins.

### Inferences
- **Do not plan around monthly chemical auto-ship.** Usage math (1–4 oz per wash, about 26 washes/year for a frequent home washer) implies 60–120+ day cadences. Most Americans use professional washes (83–89% by ICA data, older). The serviceable DIY population is a minority.
- **The 20% "monthly subscriber conversion" is a 2022 vendor-PR number.** The apparent de-emphasis of subscriptions on chemicalguys.com in 2026 is a negative signal; I could not verify whether the program still runs.
- **Best-fit model:** hero kit, then a quarterly or flexible "refill" subscription (Offer A), plus microfiber as an add-on (Offer C), plus seasonal swaps to reduce winter churn (borrowing from Offer B).

### Gaps
- I found no published churn or retention data for any detailing subscription.
- I found no seasonal sales data (e.g., Google Trends or brand data on winter dips).
- I have no verified per-wash usage for car shampoos (Adam's dilution not found) and no foam-cannon soap usage.
- I did not obtain current USPS Ground Advantage / UPS Ground rates or 3PL hazmat surcharges. Shipping and pick/pack figures above are assumptions.

**Shipping liquids (USPS)**
- ORM-D was retired for ground transport on December 31, 2020. USPS revised Publication 52 to remove ORM-D, and shippers must now use the DOT Limited Quantity mark. — [USPS Link Dec 2020](https://news.usps.com/2020/12/22/hazardous-materials/); [Wikipedia ORM-D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORM-D)
- **Publication 52 §332:**
  - Limited Quantity materials may go by surface, and by air only if reclassified as Consumer Commodity. Domestic only.
  - Eligibility requires a column 8A exception in 49 CFR 172.101. The mailer must determine the hazard class.
  - Surface packages must bear the DOT Limited Quantity surface mark.
  - — [USPS Pub 52 §332](https://pe.usps.com/text/pub52/pub52c3_012.htm); [USPS Pub 52 PDF](https://pe.usps.com/cpim/ftp/pubs/pub52/pub52.pdf)
- **Third-party guides (conflicting, verify against Pub 52):**
  - Aerosols go via Ground Advantage only, with no USPS hazmat surcharge within limits. One guide gives a 24 oz per can / 72 oz per package cap; ParcelPath gives 33.8 fl oz metal / 4 fl oz non-metal (for insect repellents).
  - — [atoship 2026](https://atoship.com/blog/usps-hazardous-materials-shipping-rules); [ParcelPath](https://parcelpath.com/shipping-companies/usps/shipping-insect-repellents-with-usps/)

**Prop 65**
- Cocamide DEA (a common foaming surfactant) is Prop 65-listed. Private enforcers have pursued shampoos and soaps with 60-day notices and settlements (2013–2026 cases). These are personal-care cases; I found no car-care-specific cases. — [CA AG 2024 settlement](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/settlements/2024-03748S6814.pdf); [CA AG 2026 complaint](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/complaints/2026-00523C0817.pdf); [Rose Sheet](https://hbw.citeline.com/RS019018/Cosmetics-Firms-To-Phase-Out-Cocamide-DEA-Under-Prop-65-Settlements)

### Inferences
- **Startup cost for a 6-SKU chemical line at Renegade [ESTIMATE]:** 6 × 60 bottles × about $5–$10 = about $1,800–$3,600, before samples, freight and label iteration. Ethos at 50-unit MOQ is likely in a similar range, but pricing is quote-only.
- **Formulation choices [inference from CARB table]:**
  - Shampoo must be essentially VOC-free (0.2%).
  - A quick detailer / spray sealant marketed as an "instant detailer" must be at or below 3% VOC.
  - Solvent-based tire shines and aerosol products are the hardest to make California-compliant and to ship. Prefer water-based, non-aerosol trigger sprays.
- **Hazmat:** water-based non-flammable liquids are generally not DOT-regulated. Aerosols and solvent-based (flammable) dressings or tar removers require Limited Quantity, ground-only handling, which rules out air and slows delivery.
- **Prop 65:** require the blender to supply ingredient disclosures and avoid cocamide DEA and other listed chemicals, rather than relying on warning labels. Ethos markets Prop 65 compliance; Renegade does not state it on the program page.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the current Publication 52 quantity limits for Class 2 aerosols or Class 3 flammable liquids, or FedEx/UPS ground hazmat fees.
- I have no Renegade per-unit price tiers above the 60-bottle minimum, and no Ethos or Lab Zone price sheets.
- I have no product-level confirmation that any blender's formulas meet CARB categories. Request CARB category and VOC % per SKU in writing.
- I did not check other states' VOC rules (OTC states, e.g., NJ/VA, largely mirror CARB).
