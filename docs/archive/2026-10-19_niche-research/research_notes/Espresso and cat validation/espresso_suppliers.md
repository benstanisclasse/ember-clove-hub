# Supplier and product feasibility (Phase D and D1): Breville 54mm / De'Longhi 51mm upgrade-and-care brand, US, October 2026

Research date: 2026-10-19. Method: I read live AliExpress US and Amazon pages in the built-in browser and clicked through each SKU to get per-variant prices. I used WebFetch and WebSearch for the rest.

**Access limits (read before using the numbers):**
- **CJdropshipping** search redirected to a "Human verification" bot check at https://frontend.cjdropshipping.com/egg/cj/validation.html. I did not try to get past it, so no CJ listings were collected.
- **Alibaba.com** product pages showed a slider CAPTCHA in the browser and returned empty to WebFetch, for example https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/Dual-Head-Force-Tamper-51-53_1600191169023.html. All Alibaba figures below come from search-engine snippets of listings. I could not read them on the pages.
- **Spocket and Zendrop** catalogs need a login. I did not check them.
- **AutoDS** private-supplier listings need an account. I did not check them.
- **AliExpress prices:**
  - The price shown on AliExpress is often a "New shoppers save $X" welcome price. Repeat dropship orders may cost more.
  - The headline price in search results is the cheapest SKU, which is often an accessory such as a dosing ring or tamper mat. I clicked each SKU to get the real variant prices.
  - Delivery dates are as AliExpress showed them on 2026-10-19 to a US address.

### Inferences
- Without US-warehouse stock, a Meta/TikTok test will promise 6–12 day delivery on every hardware SKU. That is acceptable for a test but weak against Amazon Prime alternatives, such as the shower-screen kits with next-week delivery in Q3.
- Welcome-deal pricing inflates apparent margin. For modeling, use the clicked SKU prices above and add a 10–20% buffer (my estimate) for loss of the new-shopper discount and promo churn.
- AliExpress shows reviews pooled "from various sellers for similar items", which weakens seller-level quality signals. Order test units before scaling.

### Gaps
- CJ, Spocket, Zendrop and AutoDS private-supplier listings: blocked or need login. No URLs or prices collected.
- AliExpress per-store ratings ("95.3% Positive Feedback" was captured only for Shop911187022 Store) and 1–3 star review text were not systematically collected. The pages showed rating-count strips I could not label with confidence.

## Q5 (D1). Subscription fulfillment verification: Seal or Appstle → Shopify order → Dripshipper or 3PL

### Takeaway

### Flow (my inference from the above)
1. Seal or Appstle charges the card on the billing date.
2. On success, a Shopify order is created with line items for the beans (a Dripshipper product) and the care kit (a separate product).
3. Dripshipper should import the bean line. The care-kit line has to route separately:
   - **AutoDS stage:** AliExpress order via AutoDS, 6–12 day transit.
   - **Private-label stage:** a 3PL with its own Shopify app.
4. The result is two shipments and two tracking numbers on a partially fulfilled order.
5. If payment fails, no order is created, so nothing reaches suppliers. Retries continue per app settings.
6. If the customer skips, no order is created for that cycle.
7. Dripshipper charges the merchant's card at its current wholesale price. The customer's subscription price stays fixed in Seal or Appstle, so supplier price rises compress margin silently.

## Q6. Regulatory and safety: food-contact, Prop 65, FCC, batteries, CPSC

### Takeaway
- **Portafilters and anything with brass:** Prop 65 lead exposure has an enforcement history with espresso gear, so a warning or a lead-free material certificate is needed.
- **Silicone gaskets:** fall under the FDA's rubber-articles rule.
- **Electronic scales:** need FCC Part 15 authorization (SDoC with a US responsible party) before marketing. If they use coin cells, they also fall under Reese's Law (UL 4200A-2023).
- **Cleaners:** must avoid "sanitize" claims unless registered under FIFRA.

### Cited findings
- **Prop 65, espresso machines with leaded brass:**
  - A 2007 consent judgment set the warning text: 12-point type, bold "WARNING", placed on the front or top of the package — https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/judgments/2007-00242J674.pdf
  - Mateel Environmental Justice Foundation complaints alleged lead exposure from water heated in leaded-brass parts — https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/complaints/2008-00335C1280.pdf
  - A judgment against Briel-Industria ordered $460,000 in civil penalties (per search summary of OAG documents) — https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/judgments/2007-00126J960.pdf
- **Prop 65 at retail:** parts retailers carry Prop 65 lead warnings on brass portafilter bodies (Ascaso, Sanremo, La Marzocco) — https://www.espressoparts.com/products/ascaso-portafilter-body ; https://espressoparts.com/products/sanremo-oem-portafilter-body
- **AliExpress material declarations:** listings show seller-declared fields such as "High-concerned chemical: none" and "Material: Stainless Steel". These are self-declarations, not test reports — https://www.aliexpress.us/item/3256809291278050.html ; https://www.aliexpress.us/item/3256811862330693.html
- **FDA, 21 CFR 177.2600:** governs rubber articles meant for repeated food contact. This applies to silicone gaskets and tamper-mat contact parts — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-177/subpart-C/section-177.2600 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/177.2600
- **FCC, 47 CFR 15.101:** "Unintentional radiators shall be authorized prior to the initiation of marketing" through certification or a Supplier's Declaration of Conformity — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-15/subpart-B/section-15.101 . An SDoC requires a responsible party located in the US — https://www.yachtwave.com/compliance-fcc . Bluetooth-enabled scales would also be intentional radiators (my inference; not sourced).
- **Reese's Law (CPSC, 16 CFR 1263):**
  - Consumer products containing button or coin cells must comply with ANSI/UL 4200A-2023 if manufactured or imported on or after Oct 23, 2023.
  - Package warning requirements apply to products manufactured or imported after Sept 21, 2024.
  - Battery packaging must meet 16 CFR 1700.15.
  - Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/1263.1 ; https://www.intertek.com/retail/us-reeses-law-button-cell-coin-battery/
- **FIFRA:** registration is required for products with sanitizing or antimicrobial claims — https://www.americanbar.org/groups/environment_energy_resources/resources/newsletters/enforcement-litigation/fifra-antimicrobial-enforcement-litigation-wake-covid-19/

### Inferences
- **For the AutoDS test:** stainless-only SKUs (tamper, puck screen, funnel, knock box) carry the least regulatory risk. Scales carry the most: FCC, battery and lithium shipping rules, plus Reese's Law if a coin cell is used. The listed IKAPE and TIMEMORE scales are "rechargeable", which suggests built-in lithium cells rather than coin cells (unverified).
- **For private label:** request material certificates from the factory, ideally a 304 stainless declaration and a lead-content test for any brass or chrome parts. For scales, request an FCC test report, ideally with the factory or a US agent named as responsible party. Budget for a Prop 65 warning on any portafilter with brass components.

### Gaps
- I found no FDA source on stainless-steel food contact. FDA does not have a single "21 CFR" section for stainless that I could cite.
- I found no source confirming whether the specific AliExpress scales carry FCC IDs or SDoC marks.
- UN38.3 or lithium-battery shipping requirements for scales were not researched.
- I found no CPSC requirements specific to espresso accessories beyond the general rules above.
